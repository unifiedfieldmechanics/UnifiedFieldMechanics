The net

In the heaven of Indra there is said to be a net that stretches without end in every direction. At every crossing of its threads hangs a single jewel, and because the net is infinite the jewels are infinite. Each one reflects every other — and reflects the reflections in them, and the reflections in those, without a last term.

The point of the image is not the multiplicity. It is that no jewel is the source of the light, and no jewel is separable from what it shows. Remove one and every other changes.

Avataṃsaka Sūtra · elaborated by Fazang (643–712) of the Huayan school, who is said to have built a room of mirrors with a lamp and a small Buddha figure at its centre to demonstrate it to an empress.

Why each jewel is a torus

A bead of polished glass can reflect, but it cannot take part. A jewel that both receives and gives has to move: draw in, turn over, release, and do it again without ever running dry. The smallest form that sustains that is a torus — a flow that returns into its own beginning.

Each node here is a horn torus: every one of its rings passes exactly through the node's centre. That is why the middle blazes. It isn't a light placed there; it is the whole field arriving at one point.

The strand runs through the axis

The web does not stop at the jewel and start again on the far side. It runs straight through the vertical axis — entering below, leaving above — and the torus is the standing pattern that passage makes as it turns.

So the node does not contain consciousness. Consciousness is what is passing through, and the node is the shape it takes while it passes. Follow any strand upward and it threads the next jewel the same way.

Gathering and giving

Follow a single point of light. Along the underside it narrows inward and downward, converging on the axis; it passes through the core; above the core it turns and opens outward again, spilling over the crown and falling back down the outside to begin once more.

Centripetal — gathering toward the axis
Centrifugal — flaring away from it
The core, where the two are one motion

These are not two forces in opposition. They are one circulation caught at two moments of its turn — and at the centre there is no difference between them at all.

When a jewel goes nova

Every half-minute or so, one jewel stops husbanding itself. It gathers first — watch it contract and dim for a second, drawing in — and then the axis opens and it gives everything it has at once.

Watch what the rest of the net does. Not a wave rolling politely outward from neighbour to neighbour: the whole net is lit almost before the flash has finished. There is a front, and it does travel, but it crosses the entire structure in about a sixth of a second — so what you see is much closer to simultaneity than to delivery.

That is the difference between a net and a chain, and it is why the sutra insists on reflection rather than causation. No jewel had to be told. Each was already constituted by the one that gave; when it gave, they were already changed.

The ordinary ripple follows on behind, jewel to jewel at its own slower pace — the news arriving after the fact.

What this is, honestly

A contemplative model, not a physical theory. The toroidal dynamics here illustrate a very old metaphor; they are not a claim about how minds actually work, and nothing in current science says consciousness circulates this way.

What the model is good for is holding two ideas at the same time — that a thing can be wholly itself and wholly made of everything else — which is hard to think and comparatively easy to watch.
